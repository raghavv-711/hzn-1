What if?

Four ways to cut aviation's CO₂, applied to the flights in the air right now. Each uses published figures (sources below), and each aircraft is still years from service, so read these as rough upper bounds for today's traffic.

Sustainable aviation fuel

…less lifecycle CO₂ across every flight

Today SAF is about 0.6% of jet fuel. EU rules require 6% by 2030 and 70% by 2050; blends above 50% aren't certified yet.

Battery-electric

…of flights could fly on batteries

Each battery weighs as much as a full fuel tank. Certified aviation packs are near 200 Wh/kg today; lithium-based packs probably top out around 400 to 500. Lifecycle savings are 49 to 88% depending on how clean the grid is.

Hybrid-electric regional aircraft

…less fuel CO₂ across every flight

Assumes every regional jet and turboprop flight up to 1,200 km (the Heart Aerospace ES-36's hybrid range) uses about 30% less fuel, the RTX demonstrator's target.

Hydrogen aircraft

…less lifecycle CO₂ across every flight

Assumes hydrogen aircraft like Airbus's ZEROe concept (100+ seats, about 1,850 km) fly every narrowbody and regional flight in range. ZEROe has slipped to the 2040s.

Share of today's CO₂ each would avoid, at the settings above

How it's measured: flights come from the OpenSky Network's live feed, recorded every hour. Fuel uses the Fuel Horizon model (OpenAP, tuned per aircraft type; about 5% average error on published figures). CO₂ is 3.16 kg per kg of fuel burned, or about 3.84 kg counting how the fuel was made (ICAO CORSIA's 89 g/MJ baseline). Flights the network can't see, mostly over Africa and the oceans, aren't counted.
