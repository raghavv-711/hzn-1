Fuel Horizon

Every plane in the sky right now that volunteer receivers can hear, with an estimate of how much fuel each one has left and how far it could get on batteries instead.

What you're seeing

  • Each arrow is a real plane, pointing the way it's flying. Up close, they turn into airplane shapes.
  • Its color shows fuel left, battery left, or height, depending on the mode you pick at the bottom. The key explains the colors.
  • The line behind it is where it actually flew over the previous two hours.

Try this

  • Click a plane to see its airline, route, fuel estimate and more.
  • Search for a flight number, or for a country, state, city or airport to see only the traffic there.
  • Switch to Electric and drag the battery slider to see how many flights batteries could handle.
  • Drag to spin the globe. Scroll or pinch to zoom.

Good to know

  • Fuel is an estimate. Planes don't broadcast it, so it's worked out from the route and a performance model of each exact aircraft type (OpenAP, from TU Delft), tuned to match 126 published fuel figures. On flights it hadn't seen, it was off by about 5% on average. It plans fuel the way airlines do, with extra for a backup airport and 30 minutes of holding, so expect a range of about ±12%. Types it doesn't know get a rougher size-class estimate. See how the model was checked ↗
  • “Electric” is a thought experiment: each plane carries a battery weighing the same as a full tank of fuel. With today's batteries, almost no airliner could finish its trip.
  • Empty areas usually mean no receivers nearby, not no planes. Positions come from volunteers' ground stations, which each hear planes within about 400 km. There are thousands across North America and Europe but very few across Africa, much of Asia and the oceans.
  • It updates as you watch. The whole globe refreshes every couple of minutes, and the area you're looking at every few seconds.

Fuel Horizon is one half of a project. The other half is HZN-1 ↗, a chip designed to pick up planes' radio signals directly.
